Hydrophobia

Four heatwaves before August, and the flood maps have just been redrawn upward. The write-ups are short this time. The data speaks volumes.

If you ever wondered why forecasting departments always have progressive views on climate change, it's because they've been on the front line of data like this for years. We rebuild the seasonal normals every year, and every year they've moved. See the chart below:1 the numbers increase the shorter the history we observe, and the last 12 months beat the 30-year normal in every single month. July was 4.8 °C over.

This year has been another of dangerous heatwaves. By late July the UK was in its fourth of 2026. It's the first year on record to hit 35 °C in three separate months, with a provisional 37.3 °C hottest June day and amber heat-health alerts across most of England.2 So what do we have next? Heat dries the ground out, and dry ground sheds rain instead of absorbing it. The Environment Agency's new national assessment puts 6.3 million properties in England at flood risk. The biggest element isn't rivers or the coast, it's surface-water flooding from summer downpours, up 43% on the previous mapping.3

And then, what can we do to fix it, in a country currently curtailing renewable generation because the grid isn't fit to use it? Britain switched off a record ~10 TWh of wind in 2025, enough to power every home in London, and paid close to £1.5 billion for it once the replacement gas is counted.4 Hopefully, initiatives like these start to close the gap:

  • Wires: the Eastern Green Link subsea HVDC cables between Scotland and England, both due around 2029 under the accelerated transmission programme.5
  • Queue: Ofgem's connections reform (April 2025) reorders the grid queue so "ready and needed" projects connect first, aligned to Clean Power 2030.6
  • Market: the REMA decision (July 2025) dropped zonal pricing for reformed national pricing: locational signals without a seven-year market rebuild.7
  • Demand: NESO's Local Constraint Market pays Scottish consumers to turn demand up during constraint events, instead of paying wind farms to turn off.8

I'll be keeping an eye on these, and chiming in when I can help.
